# Literature notes: knowledge tracing methods relevant to RQ1 and RQ2

Scope: four original research publications, checked against publisher/proceedings or institutional author records on 2026-09-25. These establish the student-state and prediction background. They do **not** by themselves establish novelty for the RQ2 Local LLM policy comparison; recommendation/LLM papers must be reviewed separately.

| Original work | Student state / method | Evaluation in the paper | Relevance and precise limit for our RQ2 question |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Corbett & Anderson, *Knowledge tracing: Modeling the acquisition of procedural knowledge*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821), *User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction* 4, 253–278 (publisher issue year **1994**; some indexes show 1995) | Tracks the probability that a learner has mastered each production rule while using the ACT Programming Tutor. | Reviews empirical validity studies and reports prediction of test performance; the tutor uses mastery estimates to individualize exercises. | Direct precedent for using BKT state to guide instruction. It is **not** evidence that KT-based adaptive recommendation is new. The publisher abstract does not describe a same-input Local LLM versus deterministic-policy comparison. Full text was not verified because the publisher page is subscription-only. |
| [Pavlik, Cen & Koedinger, *Performance factors analysis – A new alternative to knowledge tracing*](https://doi.org/10.3233/978-1-60750-028-5-531), *Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence and Applications* 200, 531–538 (2009) | PFA adapts a data-mining model so student-by-skill predictions can support adaptive practice. | Compares PFA with two Learning Factors Analysis variants and standard KT, according to the authors' institutional abstract. | Strong precedent for connecting predictive state to practice selection. The available abstract gives no controlled Local LLM versus deterministic policy evaluation with identical state, graph and candidate set. Do not infer that no such study exists elsewhere. |
| [Piech et al., *Deep Knowledge Tracing*](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2015/hash/bac9162b47c56fc8a4d2a519803d51b3-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 28 (2015) | RNN learns a representation of evolving student knowledge from interaction sequences without an explicitly encoded domain model. | Reports improved prediction on real student data and explores interpretation/curriculum structure. | Demonstrates another student-state modeling family, but its primary comparison is **prediction**, not controlled recommendations from one fixed state and candidate set. It does not establish that our BKT state is universally best or that an LLM policy adds value. |
| [Su et al., *An XGBoost-Based Knowledge Tracing Model*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44196-023-00192-y), *International Journal of Computational Intelligence Systems* 16, article 13 (2023) | Uses XGBoost with problem/skill and other interaction features to predict the next answer, including multi-skill cases. | Evaluates prediction on ASSIST09, Algebra08 and ASSIST17; reports best AUC 0.9855 under its own feature/data protocol. | Supports XGBoost as an RQ1 predictor, **not** as an interchangeable student mastery state for RQ2. Its AUC must not be directly compared with ours because preprocessing, feature sets and evaluation populations differ. This paper's studied outcome is answer prediction, not a same-input Local LLM versus deterministic recommendation policy test. |

## Synthesis for the project

The narrow RQ2 working question remains: with a fixed BKT state, prerequisite graph and candidate set, does a small Local LLM policy improve prespecified recommendation-quality measures over a deterministic B+ policy under the same input conditions? The four sources above explain the predictive-state side and show that adaptive use of student state predates this project. None is sufficient to claim that the controlled RQ2 comparison has never been done. Complete the 5–10-paper literature table with direct work on graph-based recommendation and LLM educational agents before making a research-gap claim in the final write-up.

Publication dates, methods and reported evaluations above follow the linked original publisher/proceedings or institutional author pages. The statements about what a paper does *not* evaluate are bounded to those inspected pages; they are not claims about the whole literature.
